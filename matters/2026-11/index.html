MCD - Moving From an Industry Idea to a Regulatory Expectation

For several years, the mortgage industry has talked about the Mortgage Compliance Dataset (MCD) as an opportunity to modernize state examinations. That conversation is now changing.

California and Iowa have publicly outlined plans to begin using MCD in their mortgage examination processes. That is more than incremental progress. It marks the moment when MCD begins moving from development and industry collaboration into implementation.

For lenders & their Loan Origination Systems, this is a clear signal to get BE ready.

What exactly is MCD?

The Mortgage Compliance Dataset is a standardized electronic loan data file designed to support mortgage origination compliance examinations. It gives lenders, regulators and compliance technology providers a consistent structure for exchanging and testing loan-level information.

Today, state examination requests can vary in format, terminology and data requirements. Even when two regulators are asking substantially similar questions, lenders may need to interpret each request separately, create a new data extract, map fields, validate the information and work through exceptions.

The process is often manual, repetitive and expensive.

MCD replaces much of that custom work with a uniform data standard. The current dataset covers first- and second-lien closed-end mortgage originations and includes the information needed to support testing against federal lending requirements. It was developed through MISMO over three years with participation from lenders, state regulators, technology providers, compliance professionals and the Conference of State Bank Supervisors.

The objective is straightforward: lenders should not have to reinvent the data-submission process every time a state examination begins.

California and Iowa are putting MCD into motion

On October 29, the California Department of Financial Protection and Innovation announced its plans to implement MCD for residential mortgage origination compliance examinations.

California plans to begin using the new MCD Information Request to assess lender and loan origination system readiness for full implementation in 2027. By the end of the first quarter of 2027, DFPI expects to schedule a pilot examination through the State Examination System using MCD data, the standardized Information Request and compliance technology. California also plans to participate in a multistate effort to evaluate industry readiness.

California’s announcement also explains why this matters to regulators. Standardized data can support risk-based loan selection, allowing examiners to focus on areas with potential compliance concerns instead of relying primarily on random samples and manual “stare-and-compare” reviews.

Iowa is moving NOW!

The Iowa Division of Banking added Information Request 5002 to Iowa Mortgage Banker examinations initiated after November 1, 2026. The request directs lenders to provide electronic transaction data in the MISMO MCD format for all loans originated during the review period.

It also tells lenders to involve their LOS or compliance vendor if they need support. If a lender cannot comply, it must explain why. It’s expected that most lenders will cite their LOS provider not being able to produce MCD as their reason for not being able to comply.

Read that again. This is no longer a theoretical future use case. A state regulator is asking for examination data in MCD format now.

The Next Chapter Opens at the Winter Summit - Write it With Us!

New Orleans. New Ideas. Lasting Change.

The mortgage industry is changing fast, and so are the standards and technology that hold it together. The question is whether you will help shape those changes or read about them afterward.

This January, the MISMO Winter Summit brings lenders, servicers, regulators, GSEs, government agencies, service providers and technology providers to New Orleans for four days of collaboration. It is where ideas stop being ideas and become industry-wide progress.

Highlights of What's in Store:

•WORKSHOP | AI Governance in Practice: Lessons from Lenders and Regulators (Monday, January 11th 1:30 - 5:00 p.m.) - Sponsored by Dark Matter

Everyone is talking about AI governance. Far fewer organizations have been through an actual governance review and can tell you what it was like. This workshop takes AI governance from theory to application.

You will hear directly from lenders and regulators who have gone through AI governance reviews, and learn how they approach oversight, documentation and risk assessments. You will leave with insights you can use to strengthen your own program, whether it is mature or just getting started.

The workshop also kicks off the new MISMO AI Governance Forum. Forum participants will meet regularly to discuss industry governance updates, hear from the MISMO team about changes to FRAME, and share how they are using FRAME to build AI governance programs inside their organizations. Attendees in New Orleans will be in the room when it starts.

•NEW WORKGROUP | eNote Master Control Agreement (Wednesday, January 13 - 1:30 - 4:30 p.m.)

The eNote Master Control Agreement Development Workgroup (DWG) is building a standardized industry template to standardize the Master Bailee Agreement, or the tri-party agreement among a lender, its warehouse lender and its investor. Today every one of those agreements is negotiated separately and that burden has slowed eNote adoption across the industry.

Join the workgroup in New Orleans and help shape a document your own counsel, warehouse lenders and investors will be working from.

Plan the Summit Your Way

The Summit is built so you can choose how deep to go.

•January 12 is a full day of sessions and panels with industry leaders, with the big-picture view of where mortgage technology and standards are headed. Featured speakers include leaders from Newrez, Eustis Mortgage, Standard Mortgage Corporation, Pulte Mortgage, Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, Conference of State Bank Supervisors, ICE Mortgage Technology, First American, Urban Institute, Richey May, and the Mississippi Department of Banking & Consumer Finance.
•January 11, 13 and 14 are for rolling up your sleeves in MISMO workgroups and workshops, where the standards that power the industry's digital future actually take shape.

Come for two days or stay for all four.

The next chapter of the mortgage industry starts in New Orleans this January. Register now and be part of writing it - either in-person or virtually.

MISMO Kicks Off New AI Governance Forum

•Kickoff: Thursday, November 19, 2026 · 1:00–2:00 PM ET
•Meeting URL: https://mba-org.zoom.us/j/4687813951
•In-Person Meeting: At the AI Governance in Practice Workshop at the MISMO Winter Summit on Monday, January 11, 2027

MISMO introduces a new AI Governance Forum to give the MISMO community a place to share their experiences governing the artificial intelligence used within their organizations and to learn from peers working through similar challenges.

Much of the conversation will center on navigating an evolving regulatory landscape, including Freddie Mac and Fannie Mae's AI governance requirements and state supervisory guidance such as the Conference of State Bank Supervisors (CSBS) Artificial Intelligence Supervisory Framework. Members will share how their organizations are responding to these developments, learn from peers who are preparing for AI governance reviews, and discuss new guidance as it emerges.

The forum will also discuss FRAME, the MISMO Framework for Responsible AI in the Mortgage Ecosystem. FRAME is a MISMO member exclusive that provides lenders and vendors with tools and resources to manage the risk of AI systems within their organizations and support compliance with existing requirements. In the forum, members will hear what’s new in FRAME and, just as importantly, share feedback from both the lender and vendor perspective to help shape how it evolves. The group will be led by Cindy Patterson, Director of Business Controls with Pulte Mortgage.

Topics Include:

•GSE AI Governance Requirements: How organizations are responding to new requirements and adapting their governance practices.
•FRAME Updates and Feedback: What’s new from the MISMO team, how lenders and vendors are putting FRAME to work, and their feedback about implementation.
•Member-Suggested Topics: Bring the issues your organization is facing and help shape future sessions.

Make plans to join us!

MCD Implementation Update: California Department of Financial Protection and Innovation (DFPI) Schedules Pilot Exam by End of Q1 2027

The California Department of Financial Protection and Innovation (DFPI) (a MISMO Member) is set to become a pioneer in implementing the new Mortgage Compliance Dataset (MCD) for residential mortgage origination compliance examinations.

As a member of MISMO, DFPI has played a meaningful role in the development, adoption, and now implementation of the MCD by collaborating with lenders, technology providers and CSBS. The objective is straightforward: lenders should not have to reinvent the data-submission process every time a state examination begins.

California and Iowa are putting MCD into motion

By the end of the first quarter of 2027, DFPI will schedule a pilot examination through SES using the MCD Information Request, MCD-based data, and compliance technology vendors to test the MCD data standard and associated processes.

Iowa is moving NOW!

The Iowa Division of Banking added Information Request 5002 to Iowa Mortgage Banker examinations initiated after November 1, 2026. The request directs lenders to provide electronic transaction data in the MISMO MCD format for all loans originated during the review period. Read more here in the CSBS blog post.

This month, we’re featuring Veros Real Estate Solutions (Veros), a Corporate level MISMO member and a trusted innovator in mortgage technology, specializing in enterprise risk management and collateral valuation.

ABOUT VEROS
Combining predictive analytics, advanced data science, and deep industry expertise, Veros delivers automated solutions that help reduce risk and improve profitability across the mortgage lifecycle, from origination through servicing and securitization.

Veros’ solutions include automated valuation, fraud and risk detection, portfolio analysis, forecasting, and collateral risk management platforms. The company is the primary architect and technology provider of the GSEs’ Uniform Collateral Data Portal® (UCDP®) and supports the FHA’s Electronic Appraisal Delivery (EAD) portal. Veros also partners with the Department of Veterans Affairs to streamline the appraisal process for veterans.

Veros has actively participated in MISMO’s Automated Valuation Model Development Workgroup (AVM DWG) over the past two years, contributing to three documents: the AVM Common Confidence Score Usage Guide, AVM Testing Guidance and Best Practices, and AVM Fair Housing Testing Process. These resources help establish a more consistent approach to AVM use and testing across the mortgage industry.
